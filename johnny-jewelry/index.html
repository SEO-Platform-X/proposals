Lesson one

What Local AI Registry is, in plain English

People used to Google "jewelry store near me" and scroll through ten blue links. Now more and more of them ask ChatGPT or Google's AI answer box instead, and the AI just names two or three stores. If a jeweler isn't one of those names, that couple never even sees them.

How it works now

"For custom work, couples often recommend Store A and Store B..."

Two names. Everyone else is invisible.

AI decides who to name by reading about a business all over the internet: its Google profile, reviews, directories, news articles, its own website. When those sources are thin or disagree, AI doesn't trust the store and leaves it out.

Local AI Registry (LAIR) fixes that. Think of it as a Wikipedia page for the business. We gather everything the internet says about the store, the owner claims it for free and corrects it, and then our team pushes that accurate story out to all the places AI reads. The more places agree, the more AI trusts the store and names it.

Lesson two

How to pitch it

You don't need to understand the tech. You need to show the owner one thing: when a couple asks AI where to buy a ring, their store isn't on the list. Your phone does the selling. There are three ways in, and they convert very differently.

Warm

Best odds: about 1 in 10 sign

Jewelers who already know you. Text first, then visit.

  • Shops you've sold to or made pieces for
  • Owners you know from shows and suppliers
  • Anyone who'd pick up your call

Referral

Good odds: about 1 in 7

Someone they trust sends you in.

  • Your casting house, setter or diamond dealer
  • A jeweler who already signed
  • Ask every warm contact for two names

Cold walk-in

Lower odds: about 1 in 33

Strangers. Lower odds, but endless supply.

  • Tuesday to Thursday, first hour after open
  • Never on Saturday, that's ring day
  • Ask for the owner by name

Your unfair advantage: you're a jeweler

  • Talk shop first. Owners tune out marketing people. They listen to someone who knows the bench, casting and stones. Open with the craft, then the problem.
  • You know their pain. Couples walk in having already "researched" on their phones. You can say "I see it in my own business" and mean it.
  • Your suppliers know everyone. Casting houses, setters and diamond dealers serve dozens of local stores. One good word from them is a warm intro.
  • Your work is your card. Wear or bring a piece you made. It starts the conversation for you.

How to spot a store that can afford $3,000 a month

  • A real showroom, or more than one location
  • 10 or more years in business
  • 100 or more Google or Yelp reviews
  • Sells bridal, custom work or luxury watches
  • Carries brands like Rolex, Tacori or Verragio
  • Already runs ads or pays a marketing company

Skip these

  • Mall kiosks and chain stores like Kay or Jared
  • Repair-only or watch-battery shops
  • Pawn and gold-buying shops
  • Stores where corporate controls marketing
