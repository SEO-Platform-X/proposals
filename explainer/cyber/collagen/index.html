The short version

The bottom line

  • The site was hit by a search spam hack, a very common automated attack on WordPress websites. It is not aimed at our company or our products.
  • Its goal was to borrow our site's good standing on Google to promote Indonesian online gambling. It was not built to steal money or customer files.
  • Customers and providers visiting normally saw our regular pages.
  • Our web team is confirming the entry point, the cleanup, and whether any form or portal data was touched. Those answers are marked "Confirming" below.
How it was caught

Spotted by our team, not by a customer

This type of hack is built to hide from the people who run a website. The hidden code shows site managers, logged-in staff and regular visitors the normal page, and only shows the spam to Google. That is why it does not appear during routine site checks, and why most companies only find out weeks or months later, often after their search rankings drop.

Our team caught it within days through Google Search Console, the report Google provides on how it sees the site. Unfamiliar foreign-language searches showed up sending traffic to our pages, and the marketing and web teams moved straight into investigation and cleanup.

How the trick works

One website, three different stories

This is called cloaking. Hidden code checks who is asking for the page, then shows each visitor something different. That is why the pages looked normal to us while Google saw something else.

Why this site was chosen

This is common, and it is automated

Gambling spam networks run bots around the clock that probe millions of WordPress websites, which power a large share of the web. When a bot gets in, it plants hidden code and moves on. Sites with a strong Google reputation are the most valuable to them, because spam on a trusted site ranks fast.

In other words, the site was picked because Google trusts it, not because anyone targeted Induction Therapies.

Impact

What it touched, and what we are confirming

What we know
  • The pages customers saw looked normal.
  • The goal was search ranking for gambling, a common spam pattern.
  • The spike was short, focused on Nov 2 and 3.
Confirming now
  • When the code was planted and how it got in.
  • Whether contact form entries (names, emails, phone numbers) were accessed.
  • Whether provider portal accounts need a password reset.

Going forward

Locking it down with four new layers

The site already receives ongoing maintenance. Because this attack is designed to slip past normal checks, we are adding four layers aimed at this specific type of threat. An attacker would have to get through every one.

File change alerts

Instant alerts if any site file changes outside of planned work, so hidden code is flagged the moment it appears.

Google-side monitoring

Alerts when the site starts ranking for unrelated or foreign-language searches, the earliest sign of this hack.

Hardened logins

Two-step login for every admin account and fresh credentials across hosting, file transfer and the database.

Firewall protection

A security firewall that blocks known attack bots before they reach the site.
